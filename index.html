Vocal Health Indicators
Real-time acoustic health signals. Teal = healthy range. Amber = monitor. Rose = outside normal.
HNR
–
Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio — measures breathiness vs. tone clarity. Healthy singing: >20 dB. Low values suggest breathy or pressed phonation.
Titze IR (1994). Principles of Voice Production. Prentice Hall. pp. 279–282.
Jitter
–
Cycle-to-cycle pitch variation. Normal speaking voice: <1.04%. Elevated jitter is an early sign of vocal fatigue, nodules, or laryngeal tension.
Baken RJ & Orlikoff RF (2000). Clinical Measurement of Speech and Voice. 2nd ed. Singular. pp. 168–172.
Shimmer
–
Cycle-to-cycle amplitude variation. Normal: <3.81% (dB shimmer <0.35 dB). Elevated shimmer can indicate laryngeal tension, fatigue, or vocal fold pathology.
Dejonckere PH et al. (2001). A basic protocol for functional assessment of voice pathology. Eur Arch Otorhinolaryngol. 258(2):77–82.
Spectral Tilt
–
Energy roll-off per octave. Flatter tilt (closer to 0 dB/oct) = brighter, more forward placement. Steep negative tilt = darker or covered tone. Classical singing typically targets −6 to −18 dB/oct.
Sundberg J (1987). The Science of the Singing Voice. Northern Illinois University Press. pp. 118–124.
Health Over Session
HNR (teal) and jitter (amber). Downward HNR trend or rising jitter over a lesson may indicate vocal fatigue.
