Vocal Health Indicators
Real-time acoustic health signals. Teal = healthy range. Amber = monitor. Rose = outside normal.
HNR
–
Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio — measures breathiness vs. tone clarity. Healthy singing: >20 dB. Low values suggest breathy or pressed phonation.
Titze IR (1994). Principles of Voice Production. Prentice Hall. pp. 279–282.
Jitter
–
Cycle-to-cycle pitch variation. Normal speaking voice: <1.04%. Elevated jitter is an early sign of vocal fatigue, nodules, or laryngeal tension.
Baken RJ & Orlikoff RF (2000). Clinical Measurement of Speech and Voice. 2nd ed. Singular. pp. 168–172.
Shimmer
–
Cycle-to-cycle amplitude variation. Normal: <3.81% (dB shimmer <0.35 dB). Elevated shimmer can indicate laryngeal tension, fatigue, or vocal fold pathology.
Dejonckere PH et al. (2001). A basic protocol for functional assessment of voice pathology. Eur Arch Otorhinolaryngol. 258(2):77–82.
Spectral Tilt
–
Energy roll-off per octave. Flatter tilt (closer to 0 dB/oct) = brighter, more forward placement. Steep negative tilt = darker or covered tone. Classical singing typically targets −6 to −18 dB/oct.
Sundberg J (1987). The Science of the Singing Voice. Northern Illinois University Press. pp. 118–124.
Health Over Session
HNR (teal) and jitter (amber). Downward HNR trend or rising jitter over a lesson may indicate vocal fatigue.

Vowel Synthesizer
Drag on the pad to shape the vowel: left = dark/back (oo), right = bright/front (ee), top = closed, bottom = open (ah). This is the same chart the Live tab plots your voice on.
Two bandpass resonators on a buzzy source — a minimal source-filter voice, so it sounds vowel-like rather than human. Playing it through speakers near the mic while Listening will land the Live vowel dot in roughly the same spot (a handy self-test). Inspired by Neil Thapen's Pink Trombone (dood.al/pinktrombone).
Pink Trombone — an interactive model of the human vocal tract: drag the tongue and add constrictions to shape vowels and consonants directly. Created by Neil Thapen, used here under the MIT License (dood.al/pinktrombone). It runs entirely offline once loaded.

On iPhone/iPad: tap directly on the vocal tract to start the sound (iOS requires a tap inside the model to enable audio). If it stays silent, make sure your ringer/silent switch is off — iOS mutes web audio when the phone is set to silent.

Self-Test Suite
Generates synthetic tones and vowel-like signals with known pitch, formant, and harmonic properties (no microphone needed), runs them through the same detectors used live, and checks the output against expected values. Useful for verifying the analysis pipeline after any code changes, on any device or browser.
Results
Tolerances are intentionally generous (formants ±20–25%, pitch ±2%) because the synthetic signals are simplified approximations of real voices. A failing test indicates the detector is fundamentally broken, not that real-world readings will be off by exactly that much.
